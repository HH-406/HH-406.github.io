Craniosacral Work

Very light, very still contact at the head, spine, and sacrum — about the weight of a nickel. Clothed, unhurried, and quieter than most people expect.

Craniosacral work looks like almost nothing is happening, which is the part people ask about afterward. The contact is light — around five grams of pressure, about the weight of a nickel — and mostly still: hands resting at the base of the skull, along the spine, at the sacrum, held long enough that the body has time to settle rather than being asked to do anything. The work follows the craniosacral system, the membranes and fluid that surround the brain and spinal cord.

You stay clothed for this work. There is no oil and no pressure to speak of.

Jaelene trained in craniosacral work under recognized institute instructors and has used it in practice for decades, usually alongside other approaches rather than on its own. Whether a session leans this way is a decision made in the room, from what she reads in the body that day.

Who tends to book it. People who find ordinary massage too much — after an injury, during a stretch of poor sleep, or simply because deep work has never suited them. People often describe a session as unusually calming, and calm is the honest description of what the work is for.

First time? Come a few minutes early. Arriving out of breath and lying straight down means spending the first fifteen minutes catching up with yourself.
